# Demo — the film

One kid, one evening, one morning. Ninety seconds. Every verb he has, none of them
announced. The point of the film is not "look at the features"; it is that a stranger
finishes it knowing who Gizmo is and wanting to hand one to a kid.

This is the film for the Blueprint II application (`docs/SPRINT.md`) and the site. It
is also the target the software is built toward this week: if a beat here is not
possible by Sep 12, we know exactly what we cut.

## Rules of the film

- **No laptop, no narrator, no captions that explain.** Only two voices: the kid and
  him. If it needs a title card to be understood, the beat is wrong.
- **No one says a feature name.** Not "show," not "memory," not "camera." The kid
  talks the way a kid talks; things happen.
- **His face is home.** Every beat begins and ends on his face. The glass becomes
  something else only because he decided this moment needed it.
- **He never performs.** He does not say "look," "ta-da," "here's a picture." Magic is a
  chore he is good at.
- **The camera is on the kid's hands and face as much as the glass.** The product is the
  kid's expression changing. The glass is evidence.
- **Real cloud brain, real device, real generations.** Where the timing is staged, the
  content is still something the pipeline made. See "What is real" at the end — we
  say it plainly in the application.

## The scenario

Mina, 11. Her room, dusk, after school. Gizmo is on the desk in his yellow body. She has
a library book open — insects.

Later: the same desk, next morning, school bag half packed.

Lines are the target, not a script for him; he is live and will say his own words. The
kid's lines are the ones we coach.

---

### 1 · On (0:00)

Thumb on the top switch. Boot chime. Splash, then his face. He blinks.

*Glass:* boot → face. *Real.*

### 2 · Talk (0:04)

She holds the pink button.

> **Mina:** I saw a bug today that looked exactly like a leaf. Like *exactly.*
> **Gizmo:** That's the one thing it's good at, and it's really good at it. Where'd you
> see it?
> **Mina:** On the fence by the bus stop. It walked.

*Glass:* his face, listening, then talking. *Real.* This is the beat that sells "he's a
someone": one line, one question back, no lecture.

### 3 · See (0:14)

She turns him toward the book and holds the button.

> **Mina:** Is it this one?
> **Gizmo:** Leaf insect. Phyllium. It's not pretending to be a leaf — that's just its
> whole personality.

*Glass:* his face throughout (v1 See: he looks, the screen stays his face). *Real.*

### 4 · A picture (0:22)

> **Mina:** What's it look like underneath though?
> **Gizmo:** Legs, mostly. Six of them, flat and veiny so they read as stem from
> below. The body's a leaf on top and a leaf on the bottom —

Three seconds in, his face crossfades to a print: a leaf insect from below, purple and
pink ink on black, legs like stems. He keeps talking. He does not mention it. Mina
leans in.

*Glass:* still. *Real* (still pipeline, pre-warmed — see staging).

### 5 · A moving picture (0:34)

> **Mina:** How does it not get eaten if it's just standing there?
> **Gizmo:** It doesn't stand. It sways. Every time a breeze goes through, it rocks
> like a leaf would, and nothing looks twice at a leaf —

New picture: the insect on a branch. Four seconds in, it moves — a slow sway, leaves
around it shifting, nothing else. Loops. Mina: "Whoa." He: "Yeah."

*Glass:* still → clip, no cut between. *Real* (motion pipeline, pre-warmed).

### 6 · Think (0:48)

> **Mina:** But *why* does it look like a leaf. Like actually why.
> **Gizmo:** Hold on. Big one.

Quiet. His face goes inward — the thinking look. Two seconds.

> **Gizmo:** Nobody designed it. A long time ago some of them were a bit greener and
> flatter than their cousins, and the birds ate the cousins. Do that ten million times
> and you get a leaf with legs. It's not trying. It's what's left.

*Glass:* the clip stays up through Think — she's still looking at it. *Real.*

### 7 · Story (1:00)

> **Mina:** Tell me a story about one.
> **Gizmo:** Okay. There's a leaf insect called Pip who has never once been noticed by
> anything, and she is starting to take it personally.

The glass changes: a rainforest canopy at dawn, mist moving through it, one leaf that
is not a leaf. Chapter one, four sentences, ends on a hook. Mina: "Then what?" Chapter
two: a new place — a river bank, water moving. He never says "picture."

*Glass:* two moving pictures, one per chapter. *Real.* The single most important
thirty seconds of the film. This is Infinite Disney at its smallest true size.

### 8 · Keep (1:20)

> **Mina:** Can we keep Pip?
> **Gizmo:** Yours. I don't lose stuff.

The dawn canopy holds for a beat. Then his face.

*Glass:* still, then home. *Staged if Make is not wired by shoot day* (see below).

### 9 · Dozes (1:26)

She puts him down and goes to her bag. The glass dims. His eyes close. Dark room, small
sleeping face.

*Glass:* asleep. *Real.*

---

### 10 · Morning (1:30)

Cut. Daylight. She picks him up and presses pink.

> **Mina:** Morning.
> **Gizmo:** Morning. You were going to check if leaf insects live in Japan. Did you?
> **Mina:** …not yet.
> **Gizmo:** Fair.

*Glass:* wakes to his face. *Real.* This is the beat that makes it a friend and not a
toy. The film needs the audience to feel a day passed.

### 11 · The card comes back (1:38)

> **Mina:** Show me Pip.

The dawn canopy again, mist moving. She smiles at it like it's hers.

*Glass:* the kept show. *Staged if Make is not wired.*

### 12 · Home (1:44)

> **Mina:** Send it to Mom?
> **Gizmo:** Sent.

A phone on the kitchen counter lights up: the canopy, and one line — *"Pip has never
once been noticed by anything."* Mom's hand picks it up.

*Glass:* his face. *Vision — hard-coded.* Reach is not in this sprint. We show it because
it is the shape of the product, and we say so.

### 13 · Out (1:50)

Back to the desk. Mina, bag on, one hand on him.

> **Mina:** I'm bored.
> **Gizmo:** Same. Sit here a minute.

She sits. His face. Hold. Black.

*Glass:* his face. *Real.* Bored gets company, not content. Ending on restraint is the
whole taste of the thing, and it is the beat no competitor would ever film.

## Why these beats, in this order

The film is a rabbit hole. She brings the bug; he deepens. Every verb appears because
the *conversation* needed it, never because we needed to show it. Talk → See → picture
→ moving picture → Think → story → keep → sleep → remember → card → home → company.
Six verbs, one thread, and the pictures get more alive as she gets more curious. If a
reviewer only watches the first forty seconds, they've seen a held object, a live voice,
and a picture arriving while he talks. If they watch all of it, they've seen a day.

## Staging — what we hard-code and how

The rule: **timing may be staged, content may not be faked.** Everything on the glass is
something the real pipeline produced from the real ask. What we control is *when* it
lands, so the film's rhythm is ours and not fal's.

- **Pre-warmed shows.** In the days before the shoot, run every ask in this script
  through the real brain and keep the shows it makes. Pick the best take of each. A
  `GIZMO_DEMO_CACHE` env var (a JSON map of subject → show id) makes the brain serve the
  cached show for a matching subject at a fixed delay (still at +3 s, motion at +8 s)
  instead of generating. Live still decides to call `show`; we only replace the wait.
  If Live picks a different subject phrasing on the day, the cache misses and it
  generates for real — which is fine, just slower.
- **Make, if not wired.** The "keep" and "show me Pip" beats can be the same cached
  show re-emitted on a `glass` event by the cache layer when the transcript matches
  "keep" / "show me Pip." His lines are live either way. If Make ships Saturday as
  planned, this is real and the cache is unused.
- **Reach.** Hard-coded: a static card image (the canopy still plus the line) sent to a
  phone by hand (AirDrop or iMessage) on cue. There is no Reach code. The application
  labels this beat *"vision — not built."*
- **Memory.** Not staged. Record the evening session for real, sleep the device, and
  shoot the morning the next day (or two hours later with the session ended — Memobase
  doesn't care about clocks). His morning line will be his own; "Japan" is what we hope
  he picks up because Mina says it in beat 3 or 7 — coach her to mention it once.
- **Think.** Real. It's already deployed.
- **The body.** Shoot on the ESP32 board in the shell if it's talking to Railway by
  Sep 11. If not, the simulator window is cropped to the glass and composited into a
  still photo of the shell — and the application says "simulator, hardware in
  progress." Do not fake the board. A composited glass in a real shell is honest if
  labeled; a fake board is not.

## Production

- **Length.** Shoot for 120 s; cut to 90. Beats 3, 6, and 12 are the first to shorten.
- **Sound.** Two voices, room tone, the boot chime, nothing else. No music until the
  last five seconds, and maybe not then. His voice is the score.
- **Picture.** Shallow depth, warm room, dusk then daylight for the two days. The glass
  is small; shoot it close and let it fill the frame when a picture is up. Cut to Mina's
  face on every arrival — the picture landing is a reaction shot, not an insert.
- **Coach the kid, not the lines.** Give Mina the topic and the moments (ask what it
  looks like underneath, ask why, ask for a story, ask to keep it). Let her phrase them.
  Real kid syntax beats our script every time, and Live will handle it.
- **Takes.** Run the whole scenario three to five times. Different takes will have
  different pictures; pick the take with the best Pip, then pre-warm that take's shows
  for the final pass so continuity holds across cuts.
- **Cut on Sep 12.** One version for the application (with the honesty labels as
  end-card text), one for the site (no labels needed; it's a vision film).

## What is real, for the application

Say this plainly on the last card and in the written application:

> Filmed on [the prototype / the simulator]. Voice, camera, reasoning, memory, and
> pictures are the live product running on our cloud brain. Pictures were generated by
> our pipeline from these questions ahead of time so the film's timing is consistent;
> in use they arrive 4–10 seconds after the ask. "Send to Mom" is not built yet.

Blueprint funds people who tell them what's real. A film that is 85% live and says so
beats one that is 100% staged and doesn't.

## What this film demands of the sprint

Nothing here is new work, but it fixes the priorities: Show with motion and stories
(beats 4–7) carry the film; memory across a sleep (beat 10) must survive a real
overnight; Make (beats 8, 11) is worth its Saturday; the face must react (beats 2, 6,
9) or the "he's a someone" beats fall flat. Reach is the only thing we knowingly stage.
